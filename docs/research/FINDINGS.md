# What we have found so far

Every study is pre-registered: its JSON spec in [`studies/`](../../studies/) is committed before the run. Negative results stay on the board.

| Study | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Can spiking agents learn aggression or cooperation within one lifetime?** ([write-up](FINDINGS_SOCIAL.md)) | **No.** None of the 4 pre-registered hypotheses were supported. Attacks are untargeted, which points to an exploration bottleneck. An ablation also showed that an apparent 12.7% "aggression" came from a modelling artifact. | completed |
| **Does selfish lifetime learning undermine kin-selected helping?** (evolution, [C](FINDINGS_EVOLUTION_KIN.md), [C2](FINDINGS_C2.md)) | Selfish learning *doubled* sharing, the opposite of our prediction. The pre-registered follow-up then **ruled out crowding** as the cause, and J later traced the rise to locomotion weights weakening, not to learned sharing. Help reached relatives above chance **without any kin-recognition sensor**, and this replicated. The early "viscosity raises the innate share gene" result did **not** replicate. | completed |
| **Does spatial structure prevent extinction?** (F + F2, [write-up](FINDINGS_F.md)) | **It delays extinction; it does not prevent it.** F found 9/12 vs 1/12 extinctions, but half of those seeds repeated exploratory runs. The independent replication F2 (16 fresh seeds) found 13/16 vs 7/16, which missed the registered threshold (p = 0.066 two-sided). Well-mixed populations did die ~3100 ticks sooner. The proposed boom-bust mechanism was confounded and does not hold up before collapse. | completed |
| **Is learned sharing a reciprocity loop driven by gift rewards?** (G, [write-up](FINDINGS_G.md)) | **Only partly.** Removing the gift reward cut the learning effect by ~22%, and the rest remains. The learning effect itself replicated a third time. Two independent runs reproduced each other's numbers exactly. | completed |
| **Does learning strengthen every social action, rewarded or not?** (I, [write-up](FINDINGS_I.md)) | **Partly.** Learning raised signalling ~3.5× even though signalling only costs energy. Crediting only the executed action removed about half of that rise and ~40% of the sharing rise, but none of the attack rise. Every input weight fell over a lifetime, and locomotion fell the most. | completed |
| **Is the "learned" social behaviour a side effect of weaker locomotion?** (J, [write-up](FINDINGS_J.md)) | **Yes, as far as tested.** With locomotion inputs frozen and social inputs still learning, attack, signal and share rates fell to no-learning levels (attacks 0.013 vs 0.013; learning alone gave 0.112). The rise in sharing and aggression seen since C comes from locomotion learning, not social learning. | completed |
| **Is it the *weakening* of locomotion, or other foraging changes?** (K, [write-up](FINDINGS_K.md)) | **The weakening.** Locomotion still learned but its total drive was held fixed, and 80–95% of the learned rise in attacking, signalling and sharing disappeared. Exploratory probe and literature: the scalar reward critic leaves the known R-STDP bias term (Frémaux et al. 2010), which depresses the busiest pathways. | completed |
| **Do negative reward errors drive it?** (P, [write-up](FINDINGS_P.md)) | **Yes.** Ignoring negative RPEs reversed the drift (locomotion +73%) and dropped social actions to or below no-learning levels. Not pre-registered: learning agents ate **~31% less** than non-learning ones. | completed |
| **Does a per-state critic remove the side effect?** (R, [write-up](FINDINGS_R.md)) | **Mostly.** Keeping the reward expectation per sensor state (the correction from Frémaux et al. 2010) stopped locomotion weakening and removed 58–76% of the learned rise in attacks, signals and sharing. Foraging improved by about half the gap, but learners still ate ~15% less than non-learners. | completed |
| **Can any learning rule make lifetime learning pay?** (T, [write-up](FINDINGS_T.md)) | **Not yet.** R replicated: the per-state critic halved the foraging cost. Adding chosen-only credit (−12%) or a 4× lower learning rate (−7%) narrowed the gap further, but learners still ate less than fixed-wiring agents. So far, less learning is always better. | completed |
| **What happens before well-mixed populations collapse?** (L, [write-up](FINDINGS_L.md)) | Before any extinction, well-mixed populations had a **lower birth rate** and more starvation deaths. Killing is the main cause of death everywhere (58–67%). Third replication of earlier extinction in well-mixed worlds (−3900 ticks). | completed |
| **Does viscosity help through kinship or density?** (Q, [write-up](FINDINGS_Q.md)) | **Inconclusive** by the pre-registered rule: no survival or birth-rate test supported. Not pre-registered: newborns placed beside a non-relative went extinct in 11/12 runs vs 3/12 beside their parent (Fisher p = 0.003), so local density alone does not protect. **Q2 replicated this as a pre-registered test** (12/12 vs 6/12, [write-up](FINDINGS_Q2.md)). Strangers did not over-kill newborns; parents did: 42% of killings in viscous worlds are parents killing their own offspring, which recovers energy, like filial cannibalism. **Q3 falsified that recycling explanation** ([write-up](FINDINGS_Q3.md)): removing the energy gain lowered births but did not shorten survival. Pooled over Q–Q3, stranger placement gave 34/36 vs 13/36 extinct. **Q4** ([write-up](FINDINGS_Q4.md)): moving newborns just 0.15 from the parent raised extinctions from 2/12 to 8/12, and 0.45 was no worse, so gradual spatial spread is not the cause. **Q5** ([write-up](FINDINGS_Q5.md)): it is the parent specifically. Newborns placed beside another relative went extinct 10/12, like strangers (12/12), against 2/12 beside the parent. **Q6** ([write-up](FINDINGS_Q6.md)) ruled out parental culling: with filial attacks made ineffective, protection stayed (2/12 vs 3/12). **Q7** ([write-up](FINDINGS_Q7.md)) ruled out the aggression sink: redirecting attacks on offspring to other adults cut kills of young from 65% to 41%, yet protection stayed (3/12 vs 4/12, stranger 11/12). **Q8** ([write-up](FINDINGS_Q8.md)): blocking attacks and sharing between parent and offspring in both directions did not remove it either (5/12 vs 4/12, stranger 10/12). Q8 cannot rule out a broad sink, since blocked attacks still miss unrelated adults. **Q9** ([write-up](FINDINGS_Q9.md)) redirected attacks within the family both ways: kills of kin fell to the stranger level, extinctions rose to 6/12 vs 4/12 (not significant; stranger 11/12). Across Q6–Q9, changing where family attacks land gave 16/48 extinct vs 15/48. Pooled over eight studies, stranger placement went extinct 88/96 vs 30/96 beside the parent. **AD** ([write-up](FINDINGS_AD.md)) ruled out sensing: with the parent invisible to its offspring's sensors, extinctions were 5/12 (and 6/12 when parent and offspring could not see each other) vs 6/12 with a visible parent (stranger 11/12). Pooled over nine studies: 99/108 vs 36/108. **AG** ([write-up](FINDINGS_AG.md)) ruled out birth-site quality: beside the richest stranger, 11/12 went extinct, the same as beside a random stranger (parent 7/12). Pooled over ten studies: 110/120 vs 43/120. **AI** ([write-up](FINDINGS_AI.md)) is the first intervention to remove the protection: newborns beside their parent but carrying a random unrelated agent's genome went extinct 10/12 vs 4/12 with their own genome (Fisher p = 0.036; stranger 9/12). Protection needs both the parent's genome and the parent's side. **AJ** ([write-up](FINDINGS_AJ.md)) replicated it (random donor 9/12 vs own genome 4/12; pooled 19/24 vs 8/24) and found that a successful donor's genome partly restores survival (+4354 ticks, 7/12 extinct), so selection contributes; whether clustering in space also matters is unresolved. | completed |
| **Do signals evolve meaning?** (M, [write-up](FINDINGS_M.md)) | **No.** Signals carry ~0.0002 bits about food whether or not anyone can hear them. Deaf controls are just as "significant" (12/12 G-tests), so significance came from shared sensory input, not communication. | completed |
| **Do signals gain meaning when food needs a partner?** (S, [write-up](FINDINGS_S.md)) | **No.** With a partner rule and directional hearing, signals still carried ~0.0002 bits about food, the same as deaf controls. A follow-up pilot showed the rule did bind (~42% of reached food blocked), so this is a genuine negative result. | completed |
| **Lamarckian vs Darwinian inheritance** (N, [write-up](FINDINGS_N.md)) | The environment-shift test failed its manipulation check. Not pre-registered: Lamarckian populations attacked 2–3× more and went extinct in 9/16 vs 2/16 runs, consistent with inherited learning bias. **N2 replicated this as a pre-registered test** ([write-up](FINDINGS_N2.md)): 9/12 vs 1/12 extinct (p = 0.003). The per-state critic from R did not prevent it. N3 (repeated left/right flips) failed its manipulation check again, since flips did not lower foraging, but Lamarckian populations died sooner a third time. | completed |
| **Can Q-learners invade an SNN population?** (O, [write-up](FINDINGS_O.md)) | **No advantage either way.** A one-seed pilot had suggested otherwise. Not pre-registered: Q-learner lineages always ended fixed or lost (12/12 vs 4/12 for neutral labels), so fate was all-or-nothing. | completed |
| **Does an aggression gene evolve under scarcity?** (D + D2, [write-up](FINDINGS_D.md)) | **No.** D's apparent rise (+0.75) did not replicate in D2, where the gene did not differ from a neutral marker. Attack behaviour never changed. The first result was chance or drift. | completed |
| **Baldwin effect vs hiding effect** (E, [write-up](FINDINGS_E.md)): does learning speed up or shield the evolution of innate ability? | **Hiding effect.** Without learning, evolution more than doubled innate foraging (+5.5 [3.3, 7.6]). With learning, innate ability stayed near founder level (−4.4 [−7.0, −1.5] vs no learning), even across more generations. | completed |
| **Do evolving agents use their senses?** (V, [write-up](FINDINGS_V.md)) | No, not measurably. Scrambling the food and hazard sensors every tick changed neither the population food rate (+0.0015 [−0.0007, 0.0038]) nor evolved innate foraging, and populations that evolved scrambled reached the same innate score. This explains why three environment-shift tests failed. Exploratory probes trace it to the decision dynamics: action choice locks in, and a decision lasts 8 ms against a 20 ms membrane time constant. | completed |
| **Can lifetime learning pay at all?** (W, [write-up](FINDINGS_W.md)) | **Yes, once decisions are fixed.** With 32 ms decisions, state cleared before each decision, and normalised input weights, all 12 trained single agents ate 15–22 food per episode in unseen worlds, against 3.25 untrained and 2.75 with scrambled senses (≈ 5.7×). Each of the three fixes was necessary. With the default dynamics, learning did not raise foraging (−0.69 [−1.65, 0.32]); the earlier single-agent "+40%" did not replicate. In social evolving worlds the fixed dynamics led to mass killing (pilot), so that is open. | completed |
| **Does learning pay in evolving populations with the fixed dynamics?** (X, [write-up](FINDINGS_X.md)) | **No.** Normalising each neuron to its own innate input sum stopped extinction (0/30 fixed-dynamics runs died out), but learning still did not raise foraging (+0.0020 [−0.0028, 0.0062] food per agent-tick) or use the senses. The fixed dynamics themselves cut foraging by ~30% against the default and raised attacks ~15×; killing caused 65–73% of deaths. | completed |
| **How much practice does learning need?** (Y, [write-up](FINDINGS_Y.md)) | **Little.** Single agents with the fixed dynamics gained after only 150 ticks of training (+0.94 [0.45, 1.42] food per episode, +26%), ate 2.6× more after 500 ticks and 4.4× more after 1500. Every gain vanished with scrambled senses. So short lives alone do not explain why learning failed in X (rule fixed in advance). | completed |
| **What blocks learning among other agents?** (Z, [write-up](FINDINGS_Z.md)) | **Not crowding, not theft.** In a fixed group with no evolution, learning still paid among 11 non-social peers (+6.9 [3.6, 10.5]). Among social peers it did not (−2.8 [−5.9, +0.5], 9/12 seeds negative), and trained agents attacked about twice as often. Removing theft (`steal=0`) changed nothing. Candidate, untested: the social agent's extra actions and sensors. | completed |
| **Why do social agents fail to learn?** (AA, [write-up](FINDINGS_AA.md)) | **They freeze in hazards.** A social agent fails to learn even alone (+1.1 vs +13.9 for a non-social agent). Attack, share and signal do not move the body, and trained lone social agents attacked in 22% of ticks with no one around. Without hazards their learning pays (+6.2 [4.6, 7.7]) and uses the senses, though at about half the non-social gain. | completed |
| **Does removing hazards let social agents learn in groups?** (AB, [write-up](FINDINGS_AB.md)) | **No.** Among 11 social peers without hazards, learning gave +0.3 [−2.2, 2.9], against +7.3 among non-social peers. Z replicated (−2.0 with hazards). Learning more than doubled the attack rate (0.07 → 0.16) even though, as Z showed, attacks need not pay for this to happen. | completed |
| **Does switching off the frustration drive let social agents learn in groups?** (AC, [write-up](FINDINGS_AC.md)) | **No.** Without the frustration-to-attack drive, learning among 11 social peers gave +0.6 [−2.1, 3.6] (with it +0.4; non-social peers +10.3). Learning still raised the attack rate by the same amount (0.016 → 0.101). Learning raises frustration in every group, including non-social ones where it pays. A probe that switched the drive off only at test did not hold. | completed |
| **Which input weights carry the learned rise in attacks?** (AE, [write-up](FINDINGS_AE.md)) | **The movement neurons'.** With only the movement inputs learning, the attack rate rose from 0.059 to 0.255 and learning lost food (−4.3); freezing the movement inputs removed the rise (0.024) and learning paid (+2.6 [1.7, 3.5]), though not more than learning everything (+3.5). The attack neurons need not learn for attacks to rise. | completed |
| **Does the shaping reward cause failed social learning?** (AF, [write-up](FINDINGS_AF.md)) | **Yes.** Replacing alignment shaping with approach shaping turned the learning gain among 11 social peers from −1.1 to +4.9 [3.6, 6.1] (12/12 seeds positive) and removed the learned rise in attacks. Approach shaping is weak for a solo agent (+1.9), so neither shaping is best everywhere. | completed |
| **Can evolution design the learning reward?** (AH, [write-up](FINDINGS_AH.md)) | **Partly.** With heritable teacher weights, evolving populations survived about 3100 ticks longer than with the designer's reward (+3089 [62, 6131]; 7/12 vs 9/12 extinct) but did not eat more. Against the prediction from AF, evolution turned the approach-shaping weight down (−4.8 beyond drift, 5 surviving runs). | completed |
| **Do emotion-like internal states evolve when nothing is written in?** (AK, [write-up](FINDINGS_AK.md)) | **No, not here.** Heritable internal states with zero-initialised inputs, decays and outputs showed no persistence, scalability or valence beyond drifted genes, and letting them act lowered foraging by about 37% (0.024 vs 0.038 food per agent-tick). Functional criteria only; nothing here bears on subjective experience. | completed |
| **When hazards can only be felt, does a fear-like state evolve?** (AL, [write-up](FINDINGS_AL.md)) | **No.** With invisible hazards, so that pain reaches behaviour only through the evolved state, the pain response matched drift and responses lasting past the pain were shorter than drifted ones (−0.061 [−0.130, −0.009]). The acting state again lowered foraging (−18%). | completed |
| **Why does shaping break learning, and what fixes it?** (AM, AN, [theory](THEORY_shaping_covariance.md)) | **A one-line identity predicted it.** For potential-based shaping, Cov(Φ_t, r_t) = −(1−ρ)Var(Φ) ≤ 0, so a Hebbian three-factor rule with a scalar baseline depresses inputs that track the potential. Predicted fix, a state-dependent critic: in NeuralMind it turned the social learning gain from +0.5 to +6.7 [3.6, 9.6] with the reward unchanged ([AM](FINDINGS_AM.md)). In a non-spiking forager sharing no code ([AN](FINDINGS_AN.md)), shaping collapsed Hebbian credit, a state baseline rescued it, and policy-gradient credit was unaffected. | completed |
